When to Seek Psychiatric Care: Red Flags Professionals Often Miss

Many high-performing professionals dismiss symptoms that would prompt immediate evaluation in others. Here's how to recognize when psychiatric evaluation is the right step.

Who this is for: People in Chicago and across Illinois trying to find the right mental health care. Based on published research and years at the bedside in emergency and critical care. The practice opens in 2028 — join the notification list to hear when I am taking patients.

Jerrin Mathew, BSN, RN
Psychiatric-Mental Health Nurse Practitioner Student
3 min read · Updated November 2026

Professional success can obscure mental health concerns. If your career is thriving, it's easy to dismiss the fact that you're struggling privately. Here are red flags that suggest psychiatric evaluation could help.

Sleep Changes That Don't Resolve

Not falling asleep despite exhaustion. Waking at 3 AM with racing thoughts. Sleeping 12 hours and still feeling unrested. Sleep disruption is often the first sign that something needs attention.

Mood or Energy That Doesn't Match Circumstances

You got a promotion and feel empty. A vacation passes and you return no more refreshed. You're engaged at work but can't motivate yourself for anything else. Persistent low mood despite external success is a red flag.

Difficulty Concentrating on Tasks That Usually Engage You

If you're a reader and can't focus on books. A coder and can't hold complex logic in your head. A leader and can't follow meetings. Concentration changes often signal mood or attention concerns that respond to treatment.

Increased Irritability or Emotional Reactivity

You snap at colleagues over minor issues. Family interactions feel fraught. Small frustrations trigger disproportionate anger. This shift in baseline emotional response is worth evaluating.

Physical Symptoms Without Clear Medical Cause

Persistent headaches. Stomach issues. Muscle tension. Chest tightness. When medical workup is normal and symptoms persist, psychiatric evaluation can identify underlying mood or anxiety conditions.

Increasing Use of Alcohol, Caffeine, or Other Substances to Manage

Having drinks to wind down after work. Increasing coffee to focus. Using sleep aids regularly. Escalating substance use to manage symptoms is a sign that psychiatric evaluation could help.

Social Withdrawal Despite Busy Professional Life

Declining social invitations. Avoiding friends or family. Feeling disconnected from people around you. Anhedonia (loss of pleasure in previously enjoyed activities) often accompanies depression.

What Psychiatric Evaluation Looks Like

A comprehensive evaluation includes detailed history of mood, sleep, concentration, anxiety, substance use, and how these affect your function. The goal is clarity about what's happening and evidence-based treatment options.

What the Research Shows

Mental health conditions usually start early. A national study found that half of all lifetime cases begin by age 14 and three quarters by age 24 (Kessler et al., 2005). Yet treatment often comes much later: the same research team found that the typical delay between the first symptoms and a first treatment visit can stretch for years, and for some conditions more than a decade (Wang et al., 2005).

The most common reason people give for not getting help is not cost or access. It is believing they do not need it, or wanting to handle it on their own (Mojtabai et al., 2011). Brief, validated questionnaires such as the PHQ-9 for depression (Kroenke et al., 2001) and the GAD-7 for anxiety (Spitzer et al., 2006) can offer an outside check when you are not sure whether what you are feeling is a problem.

From the Bedside

As an EMT and an ICU nurse, I have seen many people reach care only when a problem became an emergency: the panic attack that sent someone to the emergency department, the drinking that ended in an ambulance ride, the depression that went unspoken until a crisis. Almost all of them had warning signs months earlier. You do not have to wait for an emergency to deserve help.
