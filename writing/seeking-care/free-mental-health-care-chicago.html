Free and Low-Cost Mental Health Care in Chicago

Cost should never be the reason someone in Chicago goes without mental health care. These options are free or low-cost.

For professionals in Chicago and Illinois: If you have questions about psychiatric care, evaluation process, or treatment options, consultation can help clarify your options. The practice opens in 2028 — join the notification list to hear when I am taking patients.

Chicago has more free mental health care than most people realize. Some of it is run by the city, some by nonprofits, and some is available through your employer without you knowing it.

City of Chicago Mental Health Centers

The Chicago Department of Public Health runs seven mental health centers offering free outpatient care for adults, children, and adolescents, regardless of health insurance, immigration status, or ability to pay. Services include screening, individual and group therapy, case management, and medication management, including same-day medication appointments.

  • Englewood (West 63rd Street): 312-747-7496
  • Bronzeville (43rd Street): 312-747-0036
  • Greater Lawn (West 55th Street): 312-747-1020
  • Lawndale (South Western Avenue): 312-746-5905
  • North Park (North Pulaski Road): 312-744-1906
  • Pilsen (South Ashland Health Hub): 312-743-7500
  • Roseland (East 115th Street Health Hub): 312-747-1090

Not sure which service you need? Call 312-747-1020. The Lawndale, North Park, Pilsen, and Greater Lawn centers have Spanish-speaking clinicians. The city also offers counseling hours at some Chicago Public Library branches, including Legler, Woodson, and Edgewater.

A Citywide Network of Community Centers

Chicago's Trauma-Informed Centers of Care network extends community-based mental health services to all 77 neighborhoods, regardless of insurance, immigration status, or ability to pay. Call 211 to find the nearest location.

Free Helplines

  • 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988, any time
  • NAMI Chicago Helpline: 833-NAMI-CHI (833-626-4244), call or text, weekdays 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. and weekends 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., for help finding resources in Chicago and suburban Cook County

Other Low-Cost Routes

  • Employee assistance programs: many employers offer several free, confidential counseling sessions
  • Training clinics: university and hospital training programs often see patients at reduced cost
  • Sliding-scale therapists: many private therapists reduce fees based on income; ask directly
  • Illinois Medicaid: if your income qualifies, it covers mental health care

If You Have Insurance

Illinois law requires most plans to cover mental health care on the same terms as medical care. If a claim is denied, you have the right to appeal.
