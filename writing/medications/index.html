Medication & Treatment

The questions people actually have about psychiatric medication — what starting one feels like, why one stops working, how to come off safely, and the side effects that go unmentioned until someone quietly stops taking it.

Why this section exists

Most of what patients are told about psychiatric medication happens in a fifteen-minute appointment, on the day it is prescribed, when they are least able to absorb it. The result is predictable: people stop in week two because the side effects arrived before the benefit and nobody warned them that is the normal sequence.

Prescribing is a large part of what a psychiatric nurse practitioner does, and explaining it properly is a large part of doing it well. These pieces cover the questions that come up after the appointment ends.

What this is not

Nothing here is a recommendation to start, stop, or change anything. Psychiatric medication is individual — the same drug at the same dose does different things to different people, and decisions require a clinician who knows your history. Stopping some medications abruptly is genuinely dangerous.

What this writing can do is help you have a better conversation with whoever prescribes for you, and know which questions are worth asking.
