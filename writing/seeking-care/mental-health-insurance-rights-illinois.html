Your Mental Health Insurance Rights in Illinois

Illinois has some of the strongest mental health coverage protections in the country. Most people never use them.

If your insurer has denied therapy, limited your visits, or demanded extra paperwork for psychiatric care, you may have more rights than you think. Illinois law, alongside federal law, requires equal treatment of mental health care.

Parity: Equal Coverage for Mental Health

Insurers must cover mental health and substance use treatment on the same basis as medical and surgical treatment. They cannot place limits on mental health benefits that are more restrictive than the limits on medical benefits, such as stricter visit caps or extra prior authorization.

Illinois's 2023 Law on Medically Necessary Care

A law passed in 2021 and in effect since 2023 requires Illinois health plans to cover medically necessary mental health and substance use care, and to decide what is covered using standard, accepted medical practices. Plans cannot reward staff for denying or steering care, and must make available how they review claims.

Illinois Enforces It

In 2020, the Illinois Department of Insurance fined five insurers more than $2 million combined for parity violations, including using inappropriate medical necessity criteria and unnecessary prior authorization.

Which Plans Are Covered

Illinois law applies to plans regulated by the state, such as individual plans and many employer plans bought from an insurer. Large employers often fund their own plans, which fall under federal parity law instead. Ask your HR department: "Is our plan fully insured or self-funded?"

If a Claim Is Denied

  1. Get the denial in writing, and ask for the specific criteria used
  2. Ask your clinician for a letter explaining why the care is medically necessary
  3. File an internal appeal with your insurer, and keep copies of everything
  4. If the appeal fails, request an external review by an independent reviewer
  5. Call the Illinois Office of Consumer Health Insurance at 877-527-9431 for free help with parity questions, complaints, and external reviews

Paying Out of Pocket

If you see a clinician outside your network, ask your insurer what it reimburses and submit a superbill. If you pay cash, federal law gives you the right to a Good Faith Estimate of costs before your visit.
