Pain & Psychiatric Care

Pain is subjective by definition — there is no test that measures it. That makes the relationship between pain and psychiatric care unusually close, and it makes being believed part of the treatment.

Why pain belongs in a psychiatric practice

Pain and mood are wired together. Chronic pain raises the risk of depression substantially; depression lowers pain tolerance and worsens outcomes. They share neurobiology, which is why certain antidepressants treat pain in people who are not depressed at all.

Beyond the biology there is the ordinary mechanism: pain restricts what you can do, restriction removes what sustains mood, sleep breaks, and the loop closes. Treating one side while ignoring the other leaves most of the problem in place.

There is also the question of being believed. Pain is subjective by definition — there is no test that measures it, only what the person reports. That makes the conditions under which someone gives that account clinically significant, not merely a matter of bedside manner.
