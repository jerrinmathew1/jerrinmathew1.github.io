OCD

OCD is not a preference for tidiness. It is a cycle of intrusive distress and the behaviours performed to neutralise it — and in some professions the compulsions look exactly like diligence.

Why OCD is missed in professionals

In medicine, law, aviation, and finance, compulsive checking can be indistinguishable from conscientiousness. The person is regarded as the most careful one on the team while privately losing hours a day to doubt that never resolves.

The forms with no visible compulsion — intrusive harm thoughts, moral scrupulosity, purely mental reviewing — are missed for years, often because people are ashamed to say the thoughts aloud and fear what disclosure would mean for their licence.

It is very treatable

Exposure and response prevention and specific medications both have strong evidence. The obstacle is almost always the diagnosis, not the treatment. This writing covers what OCD actually is, what intrusive thoughts do and do not mean, and what effective treatment involves.
