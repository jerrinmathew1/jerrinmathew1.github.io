Sleep deprivation is a modifiable risk factor for depression and anxiety. Yet many Chicago professionals chronically underslept, normalizing exhaustion as part of high performance.

How sleep deprivation affects mood

  • Mood regulation: Sleep is essential for emotional processing and regulation. Sleep deprivation makes you more reactive and emotionally fragile.
  • Depression risk: Chronic insufficient sleep is a known risk factor for depression.
  • Anxiety escalation: Poor sleep amplifies anxiety and worry.
  • Cognitive function: Impaired decision-making, concentration, and judgment.
  • Substance use: Sleep-deprived people often use caffeine, alcohol, or other substances to manage, creating additional problems.

Sleep as psychiatric treatment

Improving sleep duration and quality is often the most impactful intervention for mood. If you're experiencing depression or anxiety, sleep assessment and optimization should be part of treatment.

Strategies for better sleep

  • Consistent sleep schedule (even weekends)
  • Limiting screens 1-2 hours before bed
  • Avoiding caffeine after 2 PM
  • Physical exercise (but not near bedtime)
  • Cool, dark, quiet sleep environment

If sleep problems persist despite these strategies, psychiatric evaluation can identify underlying sleep disorders and treatment options.
