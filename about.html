About

From critical care to psychiatric care.

I am a registered nurse currently completing graduate education in psychiatric-mental health nursing, with an expected graduation in 2027.

My clinical career began in emergency medical services and continued through long-term acute care, cardiac telemetry, and neurocritical care at a Level I trauma center. In 2020 and 2021, during the pandemic surge, I worked as an ICU PCT managing intubated patients, lines and drains, supporting families on tablets for final conversations, and watching clinical deterioration and recovery in real time. That experience fundamentally shaped how I think about psychiatric illness in medical contexts.

Working with critically ill patients teaches a particular discipline: notice small changes early, treat brain and body as one system rather than two, and stay methodical when a situation stops being simple. Those habits transfer to psychiatry more directly than people tend to expect.

My long-term goal is to provide thoughtful outpatient psychiatric care to adults and professionals navigating complex mental-health and substance-use concerns — particularly the people whose external competence makes their difficulties easy to overlook.

About this writing

The articles here are educational. They are written from published sources — primarily NIMH, CDC, SAMHSA, APA, and NHLBI — and every piece lists its references.

They are not clinical advice, they do not diagnose anyone, and reading them does not establish a clinician-patient relationship. I am not currently offering independent psychiatric or prescribing services.
