The hardest part is rarely the treatment

For people who spend their working lives solving other people’s problems, the obstacle is almost never the psychiatric care itself. It is the decision to make the appointment — and the specific fear of what happens to a person in their profession who is documented as having sought it.

Physicians worry about credentialing. Attorneys worry about the bar. Executives worry about the board. Nurses worry about the same colleagues they would refer a patient to. Those fears are not irrational, and dismissing them does not help.

The writing here covers what evaluation actually involves, what confidentiality does and does not mean in practice, and how to think about the decision when your profession makes asking feel dangerous.
