Which Length?

Fifteen minutes works when your medication is working and nothing major has changed. Thirty minutes fits most visits. Book 45 when several things are going on at once, life has shifted, or a decision needs room. How often: every four to six weeks while finding the right medication, every two to three months once things are stable.

What My Training Brings to Your Care

Nine years in medicine before psychiatry, and what that will mean at every visit when the practice opens in 2028.

01

Brain and body, checked together

Years across EMS, memory care, cardiac telemetry, and five ICUs taught me to rule out the medical side first: thyroid, sleep apnea, medications, and the other look-alikes of depression and anxiety.

02

Medication and therapy, one clinician

My training includes 500 supervised hours with a psychiatrist and 125 with a psychologist. You get prescribing and psychotherapy in the same visit, not two waiting lists.

03

Careful with medications

Graduate training in advanced pathophysiology, pharmacology, and psychopharmacology, on top of years giving and monitoring high-risk medications in critical care.

04

Progress you can see

Validated scales such as the PHQ-9 and GAD-7 at the start and as you go, so you and I can see what is working in numbers, not guesses.

05

06

Steady in a crisis
