How to Find a Psychiatrist in Chicago, and What to Do While You Wait

Finding psychiatric care in Chicago can take weeks of phone calls. A plan makes it faster.

For professionals in Chicago and Illinois: If you have questions about psychiatric care, evaluation process, or treatment options, consultation can help clarify your options. The practice opens in 2028 — join the notification list to hear when I am taking patients.

Many people in Chicago give up on finding a psychiatrist after a few unreturned calls. That is not a personal failing. In a 2024 study published in JAMA, researchers posing as patients called prescribing clinicians listed in Medicaid plan directories in four cities, including Chicago. Across the four cities, only 18% of the clinicians they tried to reach accepted the insurance and offered an appointment. Directories for commercial plans are often out of date too.

Know What Kind of Clinician You Need

Psychiatrists and psychiatric nurse practitioners diagnose and prescribe. Psychologists and therapists provide therapy, and psychologists also do formal testing. Many people do best with both medication and therapy, and a primary care clinician can often start treatment for common depression and anxiety. Knowing what you are looking for narrows the search.

Where to Look in Chicago

  • Your insurance directory: filter for psychiatry, then call to confirm each clinician is taking new patients and takes your plan.
  • Your primary care clinician: ask for a referral, or whether they can start treatment while you wait.
  • Your employee assistance program: many offer free, confidential sessions and referrals.
  • Psychology Today and similar directories: filter by insurance, telehealth, and whether the clinician prescribes.
  • Hospital outpatient psychiatry clinics: Chicago's large health systems run outpatient programs, some with training clinics at lower cost.
  • NAMI Chicago Helpline: call or text 833-NAMI-CHI (833-626-4244) for free help finding resources in Chicago and suburban Cook County.
  • City of Chicago mental health centers: free care regardless of insurance or immigration status (see the related article below).

What to Ask When You Call

  • Are you accepting new patients, and do you take my insurance (or what does a visit cost)?
  • When is the earliest appointment, and is there a cancellation list?
  • Do you offer telehealth anywhere in Illinois?
  • Do you prescribe, provide therapy, or both?

Get on two or three waitlists at once, and take the first good fit.

Telehealth Widens Your Options

An Illinois-licensed clinician can see you by video anywhere in the state, so you are not limited to offices near your home or work.

What to Do While You Wait

  • Ask your primary care clinician whether they can begin treatment or bridge you until your appointment
  • Start therapy if you can get in sooner; it helps on its own and makes the first psychiatric visit more productive
  • Protect your sleep and cut back on alcohol, which worsens mood and anxiety
  • Write down your history: symptoms, past medications, and what helped
  • Know your crisis plan: call or text 988, or go to the nearest emergency department
